Privacy Policy

  • Last updated: November 14, 2026
  • Effective: November 14, 2026
  • Applies to: MattStories Studio (private desktop tool)

The short version

  • MattStories Studio (“the App”) is a private tool. It is used only by its owner (“the Owner”) to publish videos to the Owner’s own YouTube, Instagram and TikTok accounts. It is not offered to the public and not sold.
  • The App runs locally on the Owner’s computer in Poland and is reachable only from that computer (127.0.0.1). It has no server, no user accounts, no analytics and no tracking.
  • The App connects only to the official APIs of Google/YouTube, Meta/Instagram and TikTok, and only for accounts the Owner connects with OAuth.
  • Nothing is published until the Owner approves a specific video, platform and time. Test mode is the default and sends nothing to any platform.
  • Access tokens and app secrets are encrypted with Windows DPAPI. Disconnecting an account asks the platform to revoke access and deletes the stored tokens.

1Who runs the App

MattStories Studio is a private tool owned and used by Mateusz Nagler, the person behind the “Matt & Stories” channel.

  • The Owner is the App’s only user.
  • The App publishes only to the Owner’s own YouTube, Instagram and TikTok accounts.
  • The App is not distributed, offered to the public or sold, and no one else can sign up for it.
  • The App runs on the Owner’s computer in Poland. The data described below stays on that computer, apart from what is sent to the platforms (see section 8).

2How the App works

  • The App is a Python/Flask web application started on the Owner’s computer. Its built-in web server listens only on 127.0.0.1, so it is not visible to other devices on the network or the internet.
  • It helps the Owner plan short videos (YouTube Shorts, Instagram Reels, TikTok) in three daily time slots. The Owner can publish them manually or, after explicit approval, through each platform’s official API.
  • The App includes no analytics, crash reporting, advertising SDKs, third-party fonts or third-party scripts. Apart from the platform APIs listed in sections 4–6, it makes no network requests.
  • Cookies: the App sets one cookie in the Owner’s browser, for the local address only. It is a signed session cookie that holds a form-protection (CSRF) token, the temporary OAuth state value and on-screen messages. It is not used for tracking.
  • This website only hosts the App’s legal pages. It contains no scripts, forms, trackers or third-party resources.

3Data the App processes

Content the Owner adds

  • Video entries: title, per-platform descriptions, hashtag sets, notes, planned date and time slot, and publication statuses.
  • Local file paths of the Owner’s MP4 files. The App scans the configured video folders and computes a SHA-256 checksum and the duration to recognise each file.
  • MP4 files are only read. The App never edits or re-encodes them. After all three platforms are confirmed, it can move a file from the “to publish” folder to the “published” folder.
  • Optional script text and description files (studio-metadata.json).
  • Thumbnails: if the Owner picks a frame, the App uses FFmpeg to save a JPG of that frame on the computer, and stores the frame time.

Account connection data (per platform)

  • OAuth access tokens and, where the platform issues them, refresh tokens, stored encrypted (see section 9).
  • Basic account identifiers that show which account is connected: the platform account ID and display name or username, as listed for each platform below.
  • The list of permissions (scopes) granted, token expiry times and connection status.

App configuration

  • The OAuth client credentials of the Owner’s own developer apps (Google OAuth client ID and secret; Meta App ID, App Secret and Configuration ID; TikTok client key and secret).
  • Secrets are stored encrypted. The Meta credentials can instead come from environment variables, and then the secret is not written to the database.
  • Settings such as hashtag sets and whether real publishing (“live mode”) is enabled for each platform.

Logs and history

  • A history of publishing attempts and events: time, step, result, error messages, the IDs returned by the platform (for example YouTube video ID, Instagram container and media ID, TikTok publish_id) and public post links.
  • This history prevents the same video from being published twice.
  • Tokens, authorization codes and similar values are replaced with [UKRYTE] (“hidden”) before log messages are written.

4YouTube (Google)

The App uses the YouTube Data API v3 with Google OAuth 2.0 (installed-app flow with PKCE). It requests only these scopes:

  • https://www.googleapis.com/auth/youtube.upload: to upload the videos the Owner approves and to set a chosen thumbnail;
  • https://www.googleapis.com/auth/youtube.readonly: to read the Owner’s channel ID and name and the processing and privacy status of videos uploaded by the App.

Sent to YouTube after approval:

  • the video file, title, and description with hashtags;
  • tags derived from the hashtag set, and a category;
  • privacy status private with the approved publishAt time;
  • the “made for kids” declaration and the altered/synthetic content declaration;
  • if selected, the thumbnail image.

MattStories Studio’s use and transfer of information received from Google APIs adheres to the Google API Services User Data Policy, including the Limited Use requirements. YouTube features are subject to the YouTube Terms of Service, and Google handles data under the Google Privacy Policy. Access can be revoked at any time on the Google Account permissions page.

5Instagram (Meta)

The App uses the Instagram Platform (Graph API) with one of two official login options:

  • Facebook Login for Business:
    • permissions instagram_basic, instagram_content_publish, pages_show_list, pages_read_engagement (or those in the selected Meta configuration);
    • the App reads the granted permissions, the Facebook Page names, and the connected Instagram professional account’s ID and username.
  • Instagram Login:
    • permissions instagram_business_basic and instagram_business_content_publish;
    • the App reads the Instagram user ID, username and account type.

Sent to Meta after approval:

  • the video file, uploaded directly to Meta (rupload.facebook.com), or, with Instagram Login, a public video URL entered by the Owner;
  • the caption with hashtags, and whether to share the Reel to the feed;
  • if selected, the time position of the cover frame (thumb_offset).

The App reads the processing status, the publishing quota and, after publishing, the post ID, permalink and thumbnail URL.

The Instagram API has no native scheduling. The App itself sends the publish request at the approved time. Meta handles data under the Meta Privacy Policy.

6TikTok

Status: the TikTok integration is in preparation. It is waiting for configuration and, if TikTok requires it, verification of the App by TikTok. This section describes how the integration is built to work once it is enabled.

The integration uses the official Login Kit (OAuth 2.0 with PKCE) and the Content Posting API, only for the Owner’s own TikTok account.

Scopes requested

  • user.info.basic: to read the open_id and display name, which show which account is connected;
  • video.publish: to publish an approved video directly (Direct Post);
  • video.upload: to send an approved video to the TikTok inbox, so the Owner can finish posting in the TikTok app.

Data received from TikTok

  • Access token and refresh token, with their expiry times;
  • open_id and display name;
  • Creator information returned by the Content Posting API, used to show the allowed options before a post is approved. Examples: username, nickname, available privacy levels, whether comments, Duet or Stitch are disabled, and the maximum video duration.
  • Publishing status and the publish_id or post ID.

Data sent to TikTok after approval

  • The video file (uploaded in chunks), the post title/caption with hashtags, and the chosen privacy level. No privacy level is pre-selected.
  • The comment, Duet and Stitch settings, the commercial content disclosure, and the AI-generated content label, if selected.

TikTok data is used only to post approved videos and to show their status. The App does not read followers, videos, messages or analytics. While the App is unaudited, TikTok allows posts from it only as private (“Only me”), and the App enforces this. TikTok handles data under the TikTok Privacy Policy.

7How data is used

Data is used only to provide the App’s features:

  • planning and showing the connected account;
  • uploading and publishing approved videos, and checking their status;
  • preventing duplicate posts and keeping a history of what happened.

The App does not sell data, does not use it for advertising or profiling, and sends it to no one other than the platform chosen for a specific post.

8When data leaves the computer

  • Data leaves the computer only to go to Google/YouTube, Meta/Instagram or TikTok, through their official API endpoints. This happens when an account is connected, when a publication is approved, and when the App checks the status of an approved publication.
  • In test mode, which is the default for every platform, the App simulates publishing and sends nothing. Live mode must be switched on separately for each platform, and every real publication needs its own approval.
  • No other person or company receives data from the App.

9Storage and security

Data is stored in a SQLite database file in the App’s data folder on the Owner’s computer. Before a database upgrade the App saves a backup copy in data/backups. Thumbnails are stored in data/thumbnails.

  • Encryption of secrets: on Windows, OAuth tokens and app secrets are encrypted with Windows DPAPI, so only the same Windows user on the same computer can decrypt them. On other operating systems DPAPI is not available; the App then only encodes these values and shows a warning.
  • OAuth protection: each login uses a random, single-use state value. It is stored only as a hash and expires after 10 minutes. Google and TikTok logins also use PKCE.
  • Tokens in transit: API calls use HTTPS. Instagram API tokens are sent in the Authorization header, not in the URL.
  • Local-only server: the App listens only on 127.0.0.1, and its forms are protected against cross-site requests.
  • Redacted logs: tokens and codes are hidden before log messages are written.

Anyone with access to the Owner’s Windows user account could use the App and its connected accounts. No method of storage or transmission is completely secure.

10Retention

  • The App does not delete content, history or settings automatically. They stay in the local database until the Owner deletes them.
  • Pending OAuth state values expire after 10 minutes and are removed.
  • Access and refresh tokens are valid for as long as the platform allows, and are deleted from the database when the account is disconnected.
  • If a platform rejects a token, the stored access token is cleared and the App asks the Owner to reconnect.
  • Data a platform has received, such as a published video, is kept by that platform under its own policies. Deleting data in the App does not delete published posts.

11Disconnecting and deleting data

Disconnect an account

In the App, open Połączone konta (Connected accounts) and choose disconnect for the platform. The App then:

  • YouTube: calls Google’s token revocation endpoint and deletes the stored tokens.
  • Instagram via Facebook Login: asks Meta to remove the App’s permissions and deletes the stored token.
  • Instagram via Instagram Login: Meta offers no revocation endpoint here, so the App deletes the stored token and reminds the Owner to remove the App in Instagram’s settings.
  • TikTok: calls TikTok’s token revocation endpoint and deletes the stored tokens.

Disconnecting also switches that platform back to test mode. If revocation fails, the App says so, and access can be removed in the platform’s account settings.

Delete other data

  • Deleting a video in the App removes the entry, its publishing jobs and its thumbnail results, but not the MP4 file on disk. The log of publishing attempts (with platform post IDs) is kept so the same video cannot be published twice, and is removed together with the data folder.
  • Saved app credentials can be deleted on the Connected accounts page.
  • Deleting the data folder (including data/backups and data/thumbnails) removes everything the App stored.
  • Published posts must be deleted on the platform itself. The App has no delete function for them.

12Other people

  • The App is not used by, and not offered to, anyone other than the Owner, and it is not directed at children.
  • It does not collect data about viewers, followers or commenters on any platform.
  • People who see or interact with the Owner’s published videos do so on YouTube, Instagram or TikTok, under those platforms’ privacy policies.

13Rights and applicable law

The data stays on the Owner’s computer, so the Owner can view, correct, export (by copying the database file) or delete it at any time, as described above.

The App is operated from Poland. Where personal data protection law applies, this means the General Data Protection Regulation (Regulation (EU) 2016/679, “GDPR”) and Polish data protection law. The supervisory authority in Poland is the President of the Personal Data Protection Office (Prezes Urzędu Ochrony Danych Osobowych, “UODO”), uodo.gov.pl. Anyone who believes their personal data is processed unlawfully may lodge a complaint with that authority.

For questions about data held by YouTube, Meta or TikTok, contact those companies directly.
